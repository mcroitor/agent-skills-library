# Confidence Scale

Rate each key finding, not the synthesis as a whole. Start from the strongest supporting evidence, then adjust for the factors below.

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **High** | Supported by multiple independent, credible sources or by one strong primary source with a robust design; findings are consistent and directly address the question; no material unresolved conflict. |
| **Medium** | Supported by credible evidence with some limitation: few or partly dependent sources, indirect relevance, moderate methodological weaknesses, or minor unresolved inconsistencies. |
| **Low** | Supported by a single weak or indirect source, non-independent sources, opinion or anecdote, serious methodological limitations, or substantial unresolved conflict. |
| **Insufficient** | No credible evidence addresses the claim, or the available evidence is too conflicting or incomplete to support a conclusion. Report it as a gap, not as a finding. |

## Adjustment Factors

- **Evidence strength** - research design and methodological quality; for empirical work, consider sample size, controls, effect sizes, and precision.
- **Independence** - sources that repeat, summarize, or cite the same original evidence count as one line of evidence.
- **Consistency** - agreement across independent sources raises confidence; unexplained disagreement lowers it.
- **Directness** - evidence about the exact population, context, and question ranks above extrapolation from adjacent settings.
- **Recency** - matters when the field, technology, or situation changes quickly; it does not override a stronger design on its own.

## Reporting

State the level with a one-line rationale, for example: "Medium - two independent studies agree, but both use small samples [Article_A.md; Article_B.md]."
