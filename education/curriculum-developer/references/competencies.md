# The general and professional competencies

- **CG1.** The use of concepts, theories, and methods from fundamental sciences in professional activity
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can use mathematical, statistical, and logical concepts to formulate, explain, and argue problems and solutions in the field of information systems design and management.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can design information systems by applying techniques, principles, and methods from the fundamental sciences specific to the field.
- **CG2.** Operating with basic concepts from computer science, information technology, and communications
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can apply concepts from computer science, information technology, and communications for the design and management of information systems.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can develop information systems using knowledge of programming languages, environments, technologies, and design tools.
- **CG3.** Applying knowledge of legislation, economics, marketing, business, and quality assurance in an economic and managerial context
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can integrate knowledge of legislation, economics, marketing, and quality assurance into the processes of designing and managing information systems, ensuring compliance with current regulations.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can apply economic principles, marketing concepts, and managerial knowledge to evaluate, optimize, and promote projects in the field.
- **CG4.** Ensuring compliance with regulations in the field of occupational health and safety and environmental protection
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can apply legal provisions to ensure occupational health and safety, as well as environmental protection, by assessing risks and implementing preventive measures in professional activities.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can ensure monitoring and compliance with occupational health and safety standards, guaranteeing their conformity in the workplace.
- **CP1.** Specifying the operation of the information system using methods, models, and algorithms
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can apply concepts and methods from fundamental sciences to identify, formulate, and argue solutions in software product development.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can describe the components of the information system considering functional and non-functional requirements.
- **CP2.** Designing the information system in specific domains by integrating components
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can develop information systems by applying domain-specific methodologies, implementing algorithms, and using appropriate programming techniques.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can integrate information system components by selecting, adapting, and interconnecting them, ensuring system functionality, performance, and interoperability.
- **CP3.** Implementing and testing information systems in different domains
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can implement information systems by testing the system, configuring the software infrastructure, and creating prototypes.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can evaluate the performance of the information system by simulating usage scenarios, detecting and debugging errors, and automating testing processes.
- **CP4.** Maintaining and optimizing information systems
    1. The graduate/candidate for qualification can manage the maintenance of information systems by analyzing system logs, updating software, and correcting errors.
    2. The graduate/candidate for qualification can optimize maintenance processes by interacting with users to collect feedback, documenting changes, and testing new application versions.

---

- CG - general competencies
- CP - professional competencies
