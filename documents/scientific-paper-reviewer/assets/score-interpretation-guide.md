# Score Interpretation & Recommendation Guide

---

## Universal Score Thresholds

These thresholds apply **identically** whether you use unweighted or weighted scoring:

| Score Range | Recommendation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **85–100%** | **Accept** or **Minor Revision** | Paper is publication-ready or needs cosmetic edits only |
| **65–84%** | **Major Revision** | Substantial changes needed but potential is clear; resubmit after revision |
| **< 65%** | **Reject** | Fundamental flaws; resubmission unlikely to succeed |

---

## Score Interpretation by Range

### 90–100%: Excellent

**Decision:** Accept (or Minor Revision if minor issues exist)

**Typical profile:**
- All criteria scored 3–4
- No scores ≤1 requiring justification
- Clear contributions, rigorous methods, transparent reporting
- Suitable for immediate publication

**Editor commentary:**
> This paper makes a solid contribution to the field. Methods are well-described, findings are clearly presented, and conclusions are well-supported by evidence. Minor copy-editing may be beneficial.

---

### 80–89%: Good

**Decision:** Accept (or Minor Revision)

**Typical profile:**
- Most criteria 3–4; 1–2 criteria at 2
- Minor gaps in clarity or minor methodological concerns
- Contributions are clear but not groundbreaking
- Ready for publication after small revisions

**Editor commentary:**
> This paper contributes meaningfully to the literature. Please address the following minor points before publication: [list 2–3 specific items]. Once revised, the paper is suitable for publication.

---

### 75–79%: Acceptable with Minor Issues

**Decision:** Minor Revision

**Typical profile:**
- Mix of 3–4 and 2 scores; possibly one criterion at 1 with strong justification
- Certain sections need clarity (e.g., methods, presentation)
- Contribution is solid despite gaps
- Likely to be accepted after revision

**Editor commentary:**
> The paper presents interesting findings and is generally well-executed. Please revise the following sections to improve clarity: [list]. We anticipate these revisions will address reviewer concerns.

---

### 65–74%: Acceptable but Needs Work

**Decision:** Major Revision

**Typical profile:**
- Multiple criteria at 2; several at 1 with justifications
- Significant gaps in methods, data, or presentation
- Core idea is sound but execution needs improvement
- Requires substantial revisions; likely to be reconsidered after resubmission

**Editor commentary:**
> This paper addresses an important question but requires major revisions before it can be considered for publication. Please address the following issues: [list 5–7 specific critiques]. We encourage resubmission after revision.

---

### 55–64%: Borderline / Reject

**Decision:** Reject (with encouragement to revise and resubmit)

**Typical profile:**
- Multiple criteria at 1 or lower; average ~2
- Significant methodological or conceptual flaws
- May lack novelty or have unclear contribution
- Fundamental restructuring needed; consider alternative venues

**Editor commentary:**
> While the paper addresses a relevant topic, it has several fundamental issues that limit its suitability for publication in this journal:

> 1. Methodology is unclear; please see Reviewer 1 for detailed concerns
> 2. Sample size is insufficient for stated conclusions
> 3. [other issues]

> We suggest addressing these issues and considering submission to a more appropriate venue.

---

### < 55%: Clear Reject

**Decision:** Reject

**Typical profile:**
- Multiple criteria ≤1; average < 1.5
- Major conceptual, methodological, or ethical flaws
- Does not meet minimum publication standards
- No encouragement to resubmit; fundamental rethinking required

**Editor commentary:**
> This manuscript is not suitable for publication in this journal in its current form. Major issues include:

> 1. Inadequate literature review; significant recent publications are missing
> 2. Methodological flaws make results unreliable (see Reviewer 2)
> 3. Ethical concerns are not adequately addressed
> 4. Conclusions exceed what the data support

> If you wish to develop this work further, we suggest addressing all reviewer comments and considering whether a substantially revised version is appropriate for our journal's scope.

## Critical Criteria Override Rules

**Important:** These thresholds can be **overridden** by critical criteria:

### Automatic Reject (Regardless of Overall Score)

- **Criterion 22 (Ethics)** scored **0–1** → **REJECT**
- **Criterion 23 (Plagiarism)** scored **0–1** → **REJECT**

*Example:* Paper scores 75% overall but has plagiarism concerns → **REJECT**

### Automatic Major Revision / Reject

- **Criterion 7 (Methodology)** scored **0–1** (for Empirical, Systematic Review, Case Study) → **Major Revision or Reject**
- **Criterion 9 (Reliability)** scored **0–1** (for Empirical, Systematic Review, Case Study) → **Major Revision or Reject**

*Example:* Paper scores 72% but methodology is unprincipled → **Recommend Major Revision** (may become Reject if methods cannot be fixed)

### Editorial Recommendation (Not Rejection)

- **Criterion 25 (Scope)** scored **0–1** → **Desk Reject to Editor** (quality ≠ scope; editorial, not scientific decision)

*Example:* Paper scores 80% overall but is outside journal scope → **Recommend Editor Desk Reject** (paper is good, just wrong journal)

## Type-Specific Score Interpretation

### Empirical / Experimental Research

**Higher weight on:** Methodology (15%), Reliability (15%), Statistics (8%)

- **85%+:** Methods rigorous, statistics sound, results convincing
- **65–84%:** Good idea but methodological gaps; fixable with revision
- **<65%:** Fundamental methodological flaws; not salvageable without major redesign

### Systematic Review / Meta-Analysis

**Higher weight on:** Literature search (15%), Review methodology (15%), Evidence synthesis (15%)

- **85%+:** Systematic, unbiased literature synthesis; PRISMA compliant
- **65–84%:** Good synthesis but search strategy incomplete or bias risk
- **<65%:** Arbitrary selection of studies; synthesis unreliable

### Narrative Review

**Higher weight on:** Literature completeness (20%), Synthesis quality (15%), Theoretical framing (15%)

- **85%+:** Comprehensive coverage, insightful synthesis, novel organizing framework
- **65–84%:** Good review but some gaps in coverage or synthesis depth
- **<65%:** Shallow synthesis, limited novelty, poor organization

### Theoretical / Conceptual Paper

**Higher weight on:** Novelty (20%), Theory (18%), Concept clarity (15%)

- **85%+:** Significant conceptual advance; clearly articulated new thinking
- **65–84%:** Decent theoretical contribution but limited novelty
- **<65%:** Mostly restates existing theory; no meaningful advance

### Case Study

**Higher weight on:** Case uniqueness (15%), Rigor (15%), Data quality (15%)

- **85%+:** Well-documented case, rigorous analysis, clear transferability insights
- **65–84%:** Interesting case but documentation gaps or limited transferability
- **<65%:** Generic case description; limited analytical insight

## Common Interpretation Mistakes

❌ **Mistake 1:** "Score of 68% = Accept because it's above 65%"  
✓ **Correct:** 68% = Major Revision (author must submit revised version; acceptance only upon resubmission)

❌ **Mistake 2:** "All criteria average 3/4 = Score 75%"  
✓ **Correct:** Must use weighted formula for article type; equal average ≠ weighted score

❌ **Mistake 3:** "Reject a 76% empirical paper for insufficient novelty only"  
✓ **Correct:** Novelty is part of score; if novelty is your only concern, paper is still acceptable for other reasons. Reject only if novelty is **critical for the journal**

❌ **Mistake 4:** "Paper scores 85% but methodology is weak (C7=1) → Accept anyway"  
✓ **Correct:** C7=1 for empirical work → Major Revision, regardless of 85% score

---

## When in Doubt

- **Lower score than expected?** Check weights. Profile 4 (Theoretical) penalizes stats; Profile 1 (Empirical) rewards rigorous methods.
- **Paper is borderline?** Focus on critical criteria (C22, C23, C7, C9). If these pass, paper is likely acceptable.
- **Can't decide?** Ask: "If I were an editor, would I publish this?" If yes = 65%+. If no = <65%.
