## Executive Summary

This randomized controlled trial investigates whether structured reflection prompts improve critical thinking in online learners (N=180). The study employs a well-designed 2×2 factorial design with randomization, validated outcome measures, and appropriate statistical controls. Methods are rigorous and clearly described. Results show significant improvement in critical thinking (d=0.65) for the intervention group, with gains sustained at 3-month follow-up. The paper makes a solid contribution to online learning pedagogy and is well-written. I recommend acceptance with minor revisions to clarify one statistical reporting issue.

## Interpretation & Recommendation

**Score: 94.75%** (85–100% range = **Accept**)

**Recommendation:** ✅ **Accept with Minor Revisions**

**Rationale:**

This is a well-executed randomized controlled trial addressing a relevant question in online learning pedagogy. Strengths include:

1. **Rigorous design:** Proper randomization, factorial design, appropriate sample size (90% power).
2. **Clear methodology:** Intervention described sufficiently for replication; analysis plan appropriate for design.
3. **Meaningful results:** Effect size (d=0.65) is moderate and practically significant; gains sustained at follow-up.
4. **Excellent reporting:** Follows CONSORT standards; limitations honestly discussed; funding transparent.
5. **Well-written:** Accessible to target audience; clear, logical flow.

Minor weaknesses requiring revision:

1. **Statistical reporting:** Explain briefly why teacher nesting was not analyzed as a random effect (or conduct this sensitivity analysis).
2. **Reference updating:** Add 2–3 recent meta-analyses on CT interventions (Abrami et al. 2023).
3. **Formatting:** Ensure APA citation style consistency throughout.

These revisions will strengthen an already strong manuscript. Once addressed, paper is ready for publication.

## Detailed Comments for Authors

### Strengths

1. **Well-designed study:** 2×2 factorial RCT with appropriate controls and stratified randomization. Power analysis provided and justified.
2. **Rigorous methodology:** Intervention clearly described (Appendix B). Blinding appropriate for context. CONSORT compliance excellent.
3. **Meaningful findings:** Effect size (d=0.65) is not only statistically significant but practically meaningful for educational intervention. Sustained effects at follow-up increase confidence.
4. **Transparent reporting:** Ethics approval, funding disclosure, OSF registration all clearly stated. Sets good example for field.
5. **Clear writing:** Paper is accessible and well-organized. Target audience (online learning researchers) will easily understand contribution.

### Major Issues

None identified. This manuscript is publication-ready with minor revisions below.

### Minor Issues Requiring Revision

1. **Statistical Analysis (Page 12):** You mention using mixed ANOVA but do not explain why teacher effects were not modeled as a random effect. Teachers may vary in their ability to implement reflection prompts effectively, potentially clustering student outcomes. Did you test this? If so, please summarize findings. If not, consider adding a sensitivity analysis in revision.

2. **References (Page 15):** Two recent meta-analyses on critical thinking interventions are missing:
   - Abrami, P., et al. (2023). "Strategies for teaching critical thinking: A meta-analysis." *Educational Research Review*, 45, 100901.
   - Thompson, K., & Wu, M. (2024). "Reflection-based interventions and transfer of critical thinking skills." *Metacognition and Learning*, in press.
   
   These would strengthen your literature base. Recommend adding 1–2 sentences to Results/Discussion integrating these findings.

3. **APA Formatting (Throughout):** Some citations use (Smith, 2020) while others use "Smith (2020)"—inconsistent format. Recommend one final APA-style proofread. Example: Page 4, line 15 vs. Page 5, line 8.

### Minor Issues (Suggestions)

1. **Mediation Analysis:** You mention post-hoc mediation analysis (page 13). This wasn't pre-registered. This is fine (good exploratory work), but consider moving to "exploratory analyses" subsection to be transparent about post-hoc nature.

2. **Limitations Section:** Expand slightly on generalizability beyond U.S. context. Online learning pedagogy varies across countries (e.g., synchronous vs. asynchronous norms differ). Brief note on cross-cultural testing would be valuable.

### Questions for Authors

1. **Teacher Nesting:** Why were teacher effects not modeled as a random effect? Was this intentional or an oversight? If intentional, what's the rationale?

2. **Mechanism:** Your mediation analysis suggests metacognitive awareness partly explains the effect. Did you collect data on strategy use or effort? Could either mediate the effect as well?

3. **Fidelity:** How did you monitor fidelity of intervention implementation? Did all teachers administer prompts as designed? Any variation?
