# Reporting Guidelines Reference

Reporting guidelines ensure transparency and completeness in research publications. Use this reference to verify that papers follow appropriate standards for their study type.

---

## Overview: When to Use Reporting Guidelines

| Study Type | Guideline | Key Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Randomized Trials** | CONSORT 2010 | Flow diagram, randomization method, blinding, power analysis |
| **Systematic Reviews** | PRISMA 2020 | Search strategy, selection criteria, risk of bias, GRADE assessment |
| **Observational Studies** | STROBE | Objectives, design, participants, variables, bias control |
| **Animal Research** | ARRIVE | Animal source, housing, anesthesia, euthanasia, statistical analysis |
| **Qualitative Research** | SRQR / COREQ | Research team, reflexivity, data collection, analysis decisions |
| **Implementation Science** | TIDieR / StaRI | Intervention description, fidelity, implementation outcomes |

---

## CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials)

**For:** Randomized controlled trials (RCTs)

**Current version:** CONSORT 2010

### Checklist Items (Essential)

1. **Flow Diagram** 
   - Number of participants at each stage (enrollment, allocation, follow-up, analysis)
   - Reasons for loss/exclusion at each stage
   
2. **Sequence Generation & Randomization**
   - How were participants randomly assigned? (Random number table, computer algorithm?)
   - Was allocation concealed? (Critical for preventing bias)
   
3. **Blinding**
   - Who was blind? (Participants, clinicians, outcome assessors?)
   - Was blinding successful? (Were participants aware of allocation?)
   
4. **Sample Size**
   - Was a power analysis conducted a priori?
   - What was the target sample size and justification?
   
5. **Primary & Secondary Outcomes**
   - Clearly defined? Measurable?
   - Determined before data collection?
   
6. **Statistical Analysis**
   - Intention-to-treat analysis conducted?
   - How were missing data handled?
   - Effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals reported?

### Red Flags for Empirical Reviews

❌ **No flow diagram** — How many enrolled vs. completed?

❌ **Allocation not concealed** — Potential bias (clinicians could assign preferred treatment).

❌ **No power analysis** — Was sample size adequate?

❌ **No effect sizes** — Only p-values reported; can't assess magnitude of effect.

❌ **Intention-to-treat not mentioned** — Were participants analyzed in assigned groups?

### Template Reference

CONSORT full checklist: https://www.consort-statement.org/

## PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses)

**For:** Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, scoping reviews

**Current version:** PRISMA 2020

### Essential Checklist Items

1. **Protocol Registration**
   - Prospectively registered on PROSPERO (or similar)?
   - Registration date before data extraction?
   
2. **Search Strategy**
   - Databases searched? (PubMed, EMBASE, others?)
   - Search terms and Boolean operators documented?
   - Search strategy reproducible?
   - Date range of search?
   
3. **Study Selection**
   - Inclusion/exclusion criteria clearly defined?
   - Selection process (single or dual review?) documented?
   - Inter-rater reliability (kappa) reported if dual review?
   
4. **Data Extraction**
   - Standardized extraction form used?
   - Data extraction verified by second reviewer?
   
5. **Risk of Bias Assessment**
   - Bias assessment tool used? (e.g., Cochrane Risk of Bias, ROBINS-I)
   - Bias summary table or visualization provided?
   - Overall quality rating by study?
   
6. **Synthesis Methods**
   - Meta-analysis performed? How were studies combined?
   - Heterogeneity assessed? (I², Q-statistic)
   - Sensitivity analyses conducted?
   
7. **Publication Bias**
   - Funnel plot or Egger test reported?
   - Risk of publication bias discussed?

### Red Flags for Review Papers

❌ **No protocol registration** — Risk of post-hoc choices affecting results (selective reporting).

❌ **Search strategy not documented** — Reproducibility impossible. Likely selection bias.

❌ **Single-reviewer study selection** — Risk of biased inclusion/exclusion.

❌ **No risk of bias assessment** — Cannot judge quality of included evidence.

❌ **No heterogeneity analysis** — Cannot assess whether pooling is appropriate.

❌ **No discussion of publication bias** — Positive results more likely published; meta-analysis may be biased upward.

### Template Reference

PRISMA 2020 Checklist: https://www.prisma-statement.org/

PRISMA Flow Diagram: Clearly visualize inclusion/exclusion at each stage (title screening → abstract review → full-text review → final analysis)

## STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology)

**For:** Observational studies (cohort, case–control, cross-sectional)

**Items:** 22-item checklist

### Essential Checklist Items

1. **Study Design**
   - Type clearly stated in title/abstract? (Cohort? Case-control? Cross-sectional?)
   
2. **Study Population**
   - Setting and locations clearly described?
   - Inclusion/exclusion criteria explicit?
   - How were participants identified and selected?
   
3. **Variables**
   - Exposure clearly defined and measured?
   - Outcome clearly defined and measured?
   - Confounding variables identified and controlled?
   
4. **Participant Flow**
   - Number at each stage (approach → consent → analysis)?
   - Reasons for non-participation documented?
   
5. **Statistical Analysis**
   - Confounding controlled in analysis? (Stratification, regression, matching?)
   - Effect estimates with 95% CIs reported?
   - Sensitivity analyses conducted?
   
6. **Limitations**
   - Potential biases discussed? (Selection bias, information bias, confounding)
   - Direction and magnitude of bias estimated?

### Red Flags for Observational Studies

❌ **Study design not stated** — Cohort? Case-control? Impact on causal inference is different.

❌ **Confounding not addressed** — Cannot attribute associations to exposure vs. confounding variable.

❌ **Selection bias not discussed** — Who participated? Who refused? Differs between groups?

❌ **Only univariate analysis** — Confounding not controlled statistically.

### Template Reference

STROBE Checklist: https://www.strobe-statement.org/

## ARRIVE (Animal Research: Reporting In Vivo Experiments)

**For:** Studies involving laboratory animals

**Items:** 20-item checklist

### Essential Checklist Items

1. **Animal Source**
   - Strain and source of animals documented?
   - Housing conditions, temperature, light cycle specified?
   
2. **Ethical Approval**
   - IACUC or equivalent approval documented?
   - Welfare considerations addressed?
   
3. **Experimental Procedures**
   - Anesthesia/analgesia protocols documented?
   - Euthanasia method and justification described?
   
4. **Study Design**
   - Randomization and blinding used?
   - Sample size calculation documented?
   - Exclusion criteria specified?
   
5. **Statistical Analysis**
   - Descriptive statistics reported?
   - Inferential statistics appropriate?
   - Effect sizes reported?

### Red Flags for Animal Studies

❌ **IACUC approval not mentioned** — Ethical oversight lacking.

❌ **Analgesia not mentioned** — Animal welfare concerns.

❌ **Housing conditions vague** — Reproducibility compromised.

## SRQR (Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research)

**For:** Qualitative research (interviews, focus groups, ethnography)

**Items:** 21-item checklist

### Essential Checklist Items

1. **Research Team**
   - Qualifications and training of researchers described?
   - Relationship between researchers and participants disclosed?
   
2. **Data Collection**
   - Method clearly described? (Interviews? Focus groups? Observation?)
   - Setting and context detailed?
   - Audio/video recording? Verbatim transcription?
   
3. **Reflexivity**
   - Researchers' assumptions and potential biases acknowledged?
   - How might researcher perspective have influenced findings?
   
4. **Data Analysis**
   - Coding process described? (Open coding? Framework analysis?)
   - How were themes identified?
   - Is analysis auditable? (Can reader follow reasoning?)
   
5. **Interpretation**
   - Are quotes provided to support claims?
   - Negative cases or disconfirming evidence discussed?
   - Conclusions supported by data?

### Red Flags for Qualitative Studies

❌ **No reflexivity statement** — Researcher bias not acknowledged.

❌ **Analysis process vague** — Not transparent how themes emerged.

❌ **Only summaries, no quotes** — Cannot assess claims independently.

❌ **No description of negative cases** — All findings cherry-picked to support hypothesis?

## Quick Checklist: Is This Paper Following Standards?

When reviewing any empirical paper, ask:

- [ ] Has the appropriate reporting guideline been followed for this study type?
- [ ] Flow diagram present (if trial/review)?
- [ ] Randomization method documented (if trial)?
- [ ] Search strategy reproducible (if review)?
- [ ] Risk of bias assessed (if review)?
- [ ] Confounding addressed (if observational)?
- [ ] Effect sizes with CIs reported (if quantitative)?
- [ ] Limitations discussed?
- [ ] Ethical approval documented (if required)?

**If any is missing:** Major revision likely needed.

## Discipline-Specific Guidelines

**Medicine:**
- CONSORT (trials), PRISMA (reviews), STROBE (observational), SQUIRE (quality improvement)

**Nursing:**
- CONSORT, PRISMA, SRQR, COREQ (qualitative)

**Education:**
- CONSORT (educational trials), PRISMA, SRQR, GRADE (evidence quality)

**Psychology:**
- CONSORT, PRISMA, SRQR, APA Publication Manual standards

**Engineering & Computer Science:**
- Less standardized; journals vary. Check journal's author guidelines.
