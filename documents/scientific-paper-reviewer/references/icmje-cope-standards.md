# ICMJE & COPE Standards Reference

Professional standards for research ethics, conflicts of interest, and peer review conduct. Use this reference to assess ethical compliance in submitted manuscripts.

---

## ICMJE (International Committee of Medical Journal Editors)

**Website:** http://www.icmje.org/

**Scope:** Guidelines for authorship, conflicts of interest, and research conduct applicable across disciplines (especially medicine, health sciences, and increasingly other fields)

### Key ICMJE Recommendations

#### 1. Authorship Criteria

**All authors must meet ALL of the following:**

1. Substantial contributions to conception/design OR data acquisition/analysis
2. Drafting or critically revising intellectual content
3. Final approval of version to be published
4. Accountability for all aspects of work

**Red flags:**
- ❌ "Gift authorship" — Person contributed minimally but is listed
- ❌ Honorary authorship — Senior person added for prestige
- ❌ Ghost authorship — Major contributor omitted
- ❌ Corresponding author is only administrative contact, not main researcher

**Assessment in review:**
- Does author list match described contributions?
- Would each author defend all findings?

#### 2. Conflicts of Interest (COI) Disclosure

**Authors must disclose:**
- Financial: Grants, salary, equity, consulting fees, patent applications
- Personal: Family member with conflicting interest
- Institutional: Employment at company with competing interest
- Intellectual: Prior work or current beliefs

**Threshold:** Any relationship that could be perceived as biasing findings

**Red flags:**
- ❌ COI statement missing entirely
- ❌ Generic: "Authors declare no conflicts"
- ❌ Funding source not named
- ❌ Company-employed researcher — connection not mentioned

#### 3. Data & Materials Access

**Expectation:** Research data should be made available for verification

**Standards:**
- De-identified data deposited in public repository (if no privacy concerns)
- Or: Available upon reasonable request
- Or: Explain why not possible (privacy, proprietary)

**Red flags:**
- ❌ "Data available upon request" but author unresponsive
- ❌ No mention of data access
- ❌ Data "lost" or "not archived"

#### 4. Funding & Registration

**Trials:** Must be prospectively registered (ClinicalTrials.gov or equivalent)

**Funding sources:** All funding must be disclosed, even if "no specific grant"

**Red flags:**
- ❌ Trial appears to be unpublished trial result (date mismatch with registration)
- ❌ Funding withheld ("Anonymous donor")
- ❌ Industry funding not disclosed

#### 5. Institutional Review Board (IRB) / Ethics Approval

**For human/animal subjects research:** IRB or equivalent ethics committee approval required

**Documentation required:**
- IRB number and institution
- Date of approval
- Statement that informed consent was obtained (if applicable)

**Red flags:**
- ❌ No IRB statement
- ❌ "IRB exemption" claimed without justification
- ❌ Study conducted on vulnerable population (prisoners, children) without ethical oversight

## COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics)

**Website:** https://publicationethics.org/

**Scope:** Standards for publishers, editors, and reviewers to maintain research integrity and address misconduct

### COPE Core Principles

#### 1. Research Integrity

**Publication should represent honest, truthful reporting:**

- Fabrication: Making up data
- Falsification: Manipulating data or results
- Plagiarism: Presenting others' work as your own

**Red flags (Criterion 22, 23 checks):**
- ❌ Data too perfect (unlikely distributions, no outliers)
- ❌ Duplicate figures across papers (copy-paste)
- ❌ Missing experimental details (could be hiding failed attempts)
- ❌ Results inconsistent with stated methods

#### 2. Authorship & Contributorship

**Same as ICMJE above.** COPE reinforces:
- Clear author roles
- Corresponding author accountability
- Disclosure of ghost/gift authorship

#### 3. Conflicts of Interest

**COPE expects:**
- Full disclosure of financial and personal interests
- Reviewer recusal if COI exists
- Editor transparency about conflicts

**Red flag:** Paper by authors with financial stake in positive result (e.g., authors own company testing their product) — requires extra scrutiny.

#### 4. Research Ethics

**All research must follow ethical standards:**
- Informed consent
- Privacy protection
- Institutional review
- Special protections for vulnerable populations

**Red flags:**
- ❌ Research on vulnerable population (children, prisoners, cognitively impaired) without ethics approval
- ❌ No mention of informed consent
- ❌ Personal identifiers visible in data/case reports

#### 5. Peer Review Integrity

**Peer reviewers must:**
- Provide objective, evidence-based feedback
- Avoid using privileged information unfairly
- Recuse if conflict of interest
- Keep manuscript confidential

**Red flags (in review process):**
- Reviewer tries to delay competitor's paper
- Reviewer requests work to be cited (unpublished, self-interest)
- Reviewer shares content with unauthorized person

#### 6. Publication Ethics

**Editors and publishers must:**
- Handle misconduct allegations promptly
- Retract if fraud proven
- Correct errors
- Maintain transparent policies

## Applying ICMJE & COPE Standards in Review

### Criterion 22: Research Ethics (Weight varies by profile)

**What to assess:**

| Aspect | Check For | Red Flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **IRB/Ethics** | Approval documented with number/date | ❌ Missing entirely |
| **Consent** | Informed consent or exemption justified | ❌ No mention; vulnerable pop. |
| **Privacy** | Participant confidentiality measures | ❌ Names/identifiers visible |
| **Data Security** | How data protected (encryption, access control) | ❌ Vague or nonexistent |
| **Transparency** | Funding disclosed; COI stated | ❌ Funding hidden; COI missing |

**Scoring Guidance:**

- **4/4:** Comprehensive ethics statement. IRB approval, consent documented, privacy measures clear, funding transparent.
- **3/4:** Ethics addressed but minor gaps (e.g., data security not mentioned).
- **2/4:** Ethics mentioned but incomplete. E.g., "IRB approved" but no number; or "confidential" but methods vague.
- **1/4:** Ethics inadequately addressed. IRB approval vague or missing; consent not mentioned; vulnerable population concerns.
- **0/4:** Evidence of unethical conduct. No ethics approval for human subjects; potential fabrication; no confidentiality measures for sensitive data.

### Criterion 23: Plagiarism (Weight varies by profile)

**What to assess:**

| Aspect | Check For | Red Flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Attribution** | Ideas/data cited to sources | ❌ Paragraphs without citations |
| **Quotations** | Direct quotes in quotation marks and cited | ❌ Verbatim text without quotes/citation |
| **Paraphrasing** | Rewording with citation | ❌ Rephrased but uncited |
| **Self-plagiarism** | Substantial overlap with author's prior work disclosed | ❌ Same study published twice; not acknowledged |
| **Data Reuse** | If reusing own data, disclosed | ❌ Presented as "new data" |

**Scoring Guidance:**

- **4/4:** All ideas properly attributed. Quotations clearly marked. No self-plagiarism or appropriately disclosed.
- **3/4:** Generally well-attributed; minor citations missing or formatting inconsistent.
- **2/4:** Some unreferenced content; paraphrasing sometimes too close to source without citation.
- **1/4:** Significant unattributed content. Verbatim passages without quotes/citation. Possible self-plagiarism.
- **0/4:** Extensive plagiarism. Wholesale copying; ghost authorship; undisclosed self-plagiarism.

## Common ICMJE / COPE Violations

### Red Flag: Missing or Incomplete IRB Approval

**Example:** "This study followed ethical guidelines and had participant consent."

**What's missing:** IRB number, institution, approval date.

**Recommendation in review:** Request explicit IRB approval documentation. Without it, cannot verify human subjects were protected.

---

### Red Flag: Undisclosed Funding Conflict

**Example:** "Funded by NIH Grant R01-2023-1234."

**Missing:** Note that authors have financial interest if they hold equity in company commercializing findings.

**Recommendation in review:** If funding is from company with stake in results, flag heightened bias risk. Consider whether study design mitigates conflict (e.g., blinded outcome assessment).

---

### Red Flag: Authorship Concerns

**Example:** Paper has 20 authors; methods section doesn't explain how all contributed.

**Concern:** Potential ghost or gift authorship.

**Recommendation in review:** Request author contribution statement. Who did what? Would each author defend results?

---

### Red Flag: Possible Data Fabrication

**Example:** 
- Dataset has impossible values (e.g., negative income)
- Distribution too perfect (exactly normal with no outliers)
- Results suspiciously align with hypothesis despite high noise in field

**Recommendation in review:** Request raw data or statistical reanalysis. Flag for editor's consideration.

---

## Checklist: ICMJE & COPE Compliance

✅ **IRB Approval:** Documented with number, institution, date?

✅ **Informed Consent:** Mentioned (or exemption justified)?

✅ **Funding:** Disclosed? All sources listed?

✅ **Conflicts of Interest:** Disclosed (or "none")?

✅ **Data Access:** Plan stated (public repository, request, or exception)?

✅ **Authorship:** Contributions clear? All meet ICMJE criteria?

✅ **Plagiarism:** Properly attributed? Quotations marked? Self-plagiarism disclosed?

✅ **Research Registration:** Trials registered prospectively?

**If any ✅ is uncertain or missing:**
- **Minor issue:** Request clarification in revision
- **Major issue (e.g., no IRB):** May warrant Major Revision or Reject recommendation
